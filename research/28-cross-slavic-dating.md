# R28 — Cross-Slavic cognates and first-attestation dating (BL-15)

Run: 2026-10-14, Sonnet research agent. Obeys SPEC 00 (P-1 evidence, P-1.7 inferred != fact, P-3 unknown first-class).
Tags: [Q] verbatim from a page or file fetched in this run · [P] paraphrase / measured by me on fetched data · [U] unverified / not fetched.

User goal (BL-15): *«додай польські білоруські та російські корпуси і в словоформах вказуй яка форма в інших може відповідати… датування коли вона вперше виникла. ціль знайти укр форми які давніше за ті які в етимологічних поясненнях»*.

## 0. Bottom line

1. **Pivot = the Proto-Slavic entry.** en.wiktionary's `Reconstruction:Proto-Slavic/*` pages carry a structured `descendants` tree (East / West / South, with Old Ruthenian, Old Polish, Old East Slavic nodes). Measured on the kaikki Proto-Slavic file (5 461 records, 5 249 with descendants): 3 925 list a Ukrainian reflex, of which 3 141 also list Polish, 3 027 Belarusian, 3 683 Russian, **2 532 all three**, 1 971 also Old Polish [P]. Of our 12 263 lemma strings, **2 216 (18 %)** are such a descendant (stress marks stripped) [P]. This is the only licensed, machine-readable, already-aligned cognate set that exists; everything else (ЭССЯ, Starling, SStp) is print/scan or unclear-licence.
2. **Dates are the weak link and must come from several layers, not one.** Wiktionary etymology text gives «First attested in …» for only a small fraction: uk 25 / 59 877 entries match a dating phrase (my regex, loose) [P]; Polish ≈ 5 % in two 40 MB slices (471/8 748 and 125/4 694) [P]; Belarusian 8/8 147; Russian 26/6 777 (first 40 MB) [P]; but **Old Polish 2 242 of 3 346** entries have an «attested» phrase (mostly century-level, e.g. «pies … First attested in the 14th century») [P]. So the practical dated-cognate backbone is Old Polish + Polish loans + a few Old East Slavic entries; Belarusian/Russian dates must come from corpora or dictionaries, not Wiktionary.
3. **The ET-3 flag as built is almost inert** (`core/etymology.ts dictDating` only fires on «attested/borrowed in <century/year>» in the *Ukrainian* entry's own text: 25 hits in 59 877). Re-targeting it at the **donor/cognate side** (Old Polish, Polish, Old Ruthenian dates) multiplies the reachable cases by two orders of magnitude, but the claim must stay a *review prompt with an interval and a list of the sources consulted*, never «Ukrainian is older».
4. **Recommended MVP (all legal, ≈ 160 MB download, ≈ 10 MB shipped):** kaikki JSONL for Proto-Slavic (50 MB), Old Polish (21.7 MB), Old East Slavic (5.3 MB), Belarusian (35.7 MB) + streaming filters on Polish (789 MB) and Russian (901 MB) that keep only entries whose etymology templates reach `sla-pro`/`zlw-opl`/`orv`/`zle-ort` → `data/work/cognates.json` (CC BY-SA, attributed). Corpus side: TOROT (already ingested) for East Slavic ≤ 1400. Everything else (НКРЯ, ГСБМ, SPXVI, SStp, KorBa) = **link-out / manual verification only** (no bulk rights).

## 2. Cognate alignment (what maps to what)

### 2.1 Primary: Proto-Slavic descendants graph [P, measured]
For each PSl record: walk `descendants`, collect `(lang_code, word)` for `uk`, `be`, `ru`, `pl`, intermediates `zle-ort` (Old Ruthenian), `orv`, `zlw-opl`, `cu`. Key = PSl lemma. Join our lemma (stress-stripped, NFC) to PSl via `uk` descendants. Observed structure [Q] (`*xata` page): «Old East Slavic: *xáta → Old Ruthenian: xáta → Belarusian xáta, Carpathian Rusyn xáta, Ukrainian xáta → [→] Russian xáta (19th century), Czech chata, Polish chata, Slovak chata …» — the tree itself encodes **direction of borrowing** and (rarely) a date («19th century»). The node `→` marks borrowing. Wiktionary's current claim for *хата* [Q]: borrowed from Scythian, and PL/CS/SK/RU forms are **borrowed from Ukrainian** — exactly the type of case the user wants to test (is our attestation older than the date Wiktionary assigns to the donor chain?).

Limits [P]: only 3 925 PSl records have a Ukrainian reflex (Wiktionary lists the Ukrainian descendant only when an editor added it); words reaching Ukrainian via Old Ruthenian from Polish (loans), or post-PSl formations, have no PSl pivot → handled by §2.3. In the Ukrainian kaikki file only 2 379 of 59 877 entries have a direct `sla-pro` template and 1 534 go through `orv`/`zle-ort`, so **hop through Old East Slavic/Old Ruthenian entries** (their own `inh` template points to PSl).

### 2.2 Secondary: regular correspondences (candidate generation, never a label by themselves)
Used when a PL/BE/RU counterpart is wanted but no Wiktionary link exists: apply the rule, then **accept the candidate only if it exists as a headword in the target lexicon file** (otherwise show nothing, P-3). Rules carry a cite (P-1.3):
- East Slavic full-vowel (pleophony) vs West Slavic metathesis, from Wikipedia «Slavic liquid metathesis and pleophony» [Q]: «The East Slavic languages have pleophonic *CarC > CoroC, *CerC > CereC and *CalC/*CelC > ColoC»; «In Polish and Sorbian, the metathesis occurred without lengthening»; examples gorod ~ [Pl. gród type], *CalC: Russian mólot ~ Polish młot; *CerC: Russian béreg ~ Polish brzeg; *CelC: Russian molokó ~ Polish mleko [Q]. The page itself gives **no Ukrainian/Belarusian examples** [Q] — so the primary scholarly citation must be Shevelov, *A Historical Phonology of the Ukrainian Language* (1979) [U, not fetched] and Zhovtobryukh/Kulyk, Шевельов, Німчук in research/03 (cite via research/03, already in the project) [P].
- Further regular pairs to encode as data (each needs its own citation row before weight > 0, P-1.3): *ě > uk і / pl e / ru е (via research/03, 11); *tj, *dj > uk ч, дж/ж, pl c, dz; *ъ/*ь loss and vocalisation (uk о/е/∅); *ǫ > uk у, pl ą, ru у; *y/*i merger in uk и/і; *g > uk г [ɦ] vs pl g, ru г [g]; *or/*ol-type ru-be-uk identical. Orthography maps (uk ґ, pl ł, be ў…) are surface-only.
- Expected quality [P, untested]: for the ~2 500 lemmas with uk+pl+be+ru in the PSl graph, the rule set can be **validated** (precision/recall of rule-generated vs Wiktionary-given counterpart) before use — this is the project-style evidence for the rule weights. Do this before showing any rule-only counterpart.

### 2.3 Loanwords (Polish/Old Ruthenian donor)
R26 shows the donor can be inferred only by rules + etymology labels. For these, the Wiktionary chain («Borrowed from Polish X») plus the Polish entry's own «First attested in YYYY» is the direct comparison; the pivot is the Polish headword, not PSl.

## 3. Dating comparison and the «Ukrainian older than etymology» flag

### 3.1 What is compared
Three independent date objects per form, each an interval `[from, to]` with `basis`:
1. **Ours** `U`: earliest admissible attestation (P-2.3), with `witnessDate` (copy date), `compositionDate` if known (research/09), `layer` ∈ {vernacular, CS-marked, unknown} (existing CS-marker engine, research/05), `author class` (`ruthenian-rus`, …, P-2.4).
2. **Cognate dating** `C(lang)`: for each counterpart (Old Polish, Polish, Old Ruthenian/Belarusian, Russian), the earliest interval a source gives: kaikki «First attested in YYYY / the Nth century / c. YYYY‒YYYY» → `[from,to]`; century N → [100(N−1)+1, 100N] (same convention as `dictDating`), `basis = wikt-etym-text`, locator = kaikki file + word + wiktionary revision (`extracted 2026-10-03, dump 2026-09-02`). Later layers (SStp/SPXVI/KorBa/НКРЯ/ГСБМ) add *manual* records `basis = card`, `kind: P`.
3. **Etymology dating** `E`: what the Ukrainian etymology claims, either explicit («borrowed in the 17th century», already regexed) or **implied** by the donor's attestation («Borrowed from Polish X» ⇒ Ukrainian cannot be older than the first Polish X). The second form of `E` is new and is the one that finds real contradictions.

### 3.2 Flag definitions (review prompts, never corrections)
- **F1 `antedates-etymology`** — `U.to < E.from` where `E` is the explicit Ukrainian-side dating (current ET-3, keep).
- **F2 `antedates-donor`** — etymology says `uk ← donor`, and `U.to < C(donor).from`. Meaning: either the donor dating is a source gap, or the borrowing direction/time is wrong. The flag text must say so. This is the strongest «Ukrainian is older» prompt.
- **F3 `antedates-cognates`** — no direction claimed; `U.to` earlier than all consulted `C(lang)`: information only («не пізніше за…»).
- **F4 `postdates`** — kept as is.
Only F2/F3 outputs are phrased as «у джерелах, які ми переглянули, перша фіксація … пізніша»; the UI lists which sources were consulted, because **absence in SStp/SPXVI/KorBa is not evidence of absence** (corpus coverage, P-3).

### 3.3 Robustness rules (when F1/F2 may fire at all) [P, project design]
1. **Interval logic only**: fire iff the upper bound of ours < lower bound of theirs (a «14th century» dictionary date is [1301,1400]; a witness 1350 does not antedate it). Never compare midpoints.
2. **Copy vs composition**: use the *witness (copy) date* for the claim, mark `copy` when the witness is a later copy of an older composition, and show the composition date only as a note (research/09 rule). A late copy cannot support «older»; an early copy supports it strongly (autograph > copy > edition, P-2.3).
3. **Vernacular vs Church Slavonic**: a form attested only in CS-marked texts (Ostromir, Izbornyk, liturgical) is a CS word shared by all Slavs; it can antedate a Polish *vernacular* first attestation trivially. Fire F2 only if the Ukrainian attestation is vernacular, or at least has no CS marker (research/05). Otherwise emit `cs-layer` and no flag. Same rule for Polish: Latin/Old-Czech influenced Old Polish glosses are not vernacular evidence.
4. **Label period** (P-2.4): before ~1300 the author class is `ruthenian-rus` → say «давньоруська (східнослов'янська) фіксація раніша за…», never «українська». Wiktionary's own pre-1400 Old East Slavic entries (e.g. богъ c. 1056–1057) are pan-East-Slavic; the claim is about East Slavic, not Ukrainian specifically.
5. **Form-level, not lemma-level**: compare the *spelling* (орфографічна форма), and require the same sense: «First attested» in kaikki is per etymology, not per sense (known caveat in dictionaries: водка 1533 «only in medical context», the beverage sense 17th c. [Q]). If the cognate entry has multiple etymologies/senses, show F-flags only when the matched sense/etymology is the one linked from PSl.
6. **Unreliable dating text**: reject dates in «Compare/Cognates include …» clauses (they belong to another language) and in parentheticals about a different language; the regex must bind to the sentence of the entry's own language («First attested in 1496» after «Inherited from Old Ruthenian…», but «…Attested circa 1967» for лазер, loosely worded) [Q examples above].
7. **Source confidence**: dates from Wiktionary etymology text are editor-added and unsourced in the JSON (the data has no reference field I could see) [U]; mark them `P`-grade «за Вікісловником» and keep them out of anything presented as dictionary fact (P-1.7). Dates that came from SStp/SPXVI/Boryś etc. are only ever stored when we have personally verified a quote.
8. **Thin-corpus warning**: with ≤ 6 KWIC lines per form and a modest vetted-author set, a *later* Ukrainian date than the Polish one says nothing; only the earlier-than direction is informative (hence no «postdate» flag toward cognates).

### 3.4 Where this can produce real hits [P, not measured]
- Words Wiktionary glosses as Polish loans («Borrowed from Polish …; First attested in 16th c.») for which our Rus' witnesses are 12th–14th c. → either the word is native/common (not a loan) or Polish/Ruthenian in a third layer: **F2 candidates**.
- Words with Old Polish dating 14th–15th c. and our TOROT/Izbornyk attestation 11th–13th: frequent but uninformative (inherited; East Slavic obviously earlier than the first Old Polish texts, c. 14th). Present as «Pan-Slavic inherited; no flag».
- Genuinely interesting = **Ukrainian-origin or Ukrainian-mediated words that Wiktionary derives from Polish** (e.g. words in R26's -унок/-дло cases) where the Rus' record predates the Polish one — requires the donor dating, hence §2.3.

## 4. MVP (feasible now, legal)

Pipeline `pipelines/cognates.ts` (offline, `data/raw/kaikki-slavic/`, SHA-256 recorded):
1. **Fetch** (≈ 112 MB small files + two ranged/streamed big ones): ProtoSlavic 50.2 MB, OldPolish 21.7 MB, OldEastSlavic 5.3 MB, Belarusian 35.7 MB, Polish 789 MB and Russian 901 MB streamed once through a filter (keep `word, lang_code, etymology_text, etymology_templates, descendants` only where templates include `sla-pro|zlw-opl|orv|zle-ort|bor` from `pl|uk|zle-ort`). Peak disk ≈ 1.8 GB if saved, 0 if piped. Because kaikki marks all of them «will be removed», **commit the filtered output** (estimated 8–15 MB JSON, shard by first letter if > the P-4 budget) and the SHA-256s, mirroring the Pages data-shard approach.
2. **Join**: our lemma → PSl (via `uk` descendants, strip U+0301) → `{pl, be, ru, zle-ort, zlw-opl, orv, cu}` counterparts with Wiktionary sense gloss. Expected coverage 2 216 / 12 263 lemma strings (18 %) from the graph alone [P]; plus loanword join via `Borrowed from Polish X` on the uk entry (R26 count 609 pl-labelled lemmas on Wiktionary overall).
3. **Dates**: regex generalising `dictDating` over each counterpart's `etymology_text`: `First attested in (c\. )?YYYY(‒YYYY)?`, `in year YYYY`, `in the Nth century`, `Attested circa YYYY`; store `{lang,form,from,to,quote}` (Q, ≤ 300 chars, CC BY-SA attribution). Expected datable counterparts: ≈ 2 000 Old Polish + a few hundred Polish loans; BE/RU ≪ 100 per slice (measured above).
4. **Flags**: F1–F4 with rules 3.3.1–8, written to `etymology.conflict` (extend `EtymConflict` with the three new codes; ET-3 test extended: interval strictness, CS-layer suppress, copy vs composition, “Compare …” clause rejection).
5. **UI (WordView)**: block «Відповідники в інших мовах» — table lang / form / earliest date + basis badge / quote / «джерело» → in-site `/source/:id` card (BL-16); counterparts for which only a rule produced a candidate carry a «за правилом R-…» badge and the P-1.7 «не факт» label; external «перевірити» deep-links (spxvi.edu.pl index, scriptores SStp, korba.edu.pl, НКРЯ query URL, archive.org ГСБМ) as secondary «оригінал ↗» only — no scraping, no embedding of copyrighted text.
6. **Evaluation before shipping**: hand-check 40 flags (20 F2/F3, 20 matched non-flags) against Boryś / SStp / ЕСУМ; ship if precision ≥ 80 % on F2, otherwise ship F3 only.

Not in MVP: NKJP (irrelevant to first attestation), НКРЯ bulk (terms forbid), ГСБМ OCR full-text (rights unclear, OCR poor), SPXVI/KorBa bulk (no stated licence → write to IBL PAN / IJP PAN for permission; reuse that request letter for SStp scans), СлРЯ/Срезневский (no stable source found).
